## K1 · NAV
NAV (Net Asset Value) is the price of one unit of a mutual fund on a given day. The fund adds up what everything it owns is worth, subtracts costs, and divides by the number of units. NAV is worked out once at the end of every business day, so it moves up and down with the market. A lower NAV does not mean the fund is "worse"; it is just today's price per unit.
Keywords: nav, net asset value, price, unit price, daily, kimat, bhav, rate
Source: AMFI investor education, {AMFI_LINK}

## K2 · Units
When you invest in a mutual fund, your money buys units. Number of units = amount invested ÷ NAV on that day. The units you buy belong to you. A fall in NAV does not take units away; it only changes what each unit is worth today. You lose units only if you redeem (sell) them.
Keywords: units, own, kitne units, mere units, paisa doob, gaya, lost, lose, loss, nuksaan, gone
Source: AMFI investor education, {AMFI_LINK}

## K3 · Average buy NAV
In a SIP you buy units at a different NAV each month. The average buy NAV is the total amount invested divided by the total units held. It is the "average price" you paid per unit. If today's NAV is below this average, your current value is below what you invested; if it is above, your value is higher.
Keywords: average, avg nav, average cost, buy price, purchase price, kis bhav, cost
Source: AMFI investor education, {AMFI_LINK}

## K4 · Current value vs amount invested
Amount invested is the total money you have put in so far. Current value is what your units are worth today: total units × today's NAV. The difference is your gain or loss on paper. It becomes a real (booked) gain or loss only if you redeem units. Until then it changes every day with the NAV.
Keywords: current value, invested, loss, profit, gain, paper loss, notional, doob gaya, nuksaan, ghata, kam, below, down, munafa
Source: AMFI investor education, {AMFI_LINK}

## K5 · Absolute return vs XIRR
Absolute return is simple: (current value − invested) ÷ invested, shown as a %. It ignores time. XIRR is an annualised return: it takes the date of each instalment into account and expresses the result as a yearly rate. In the first few months of a SIP, even a small rupee change gets stretched to a full-year rate, so XIRR can look very large (up or down). As the SIP runs longer, XIRR usually moves less sharply. XIRR describes the past; it is not a forecast.
Keywords: xirr, return, annualised, annual, yearly, cagr, absolute return, percentage, extreme, high, negative, minus, saalana
Source: AMFI investor education, {AMFI_LINK}

## K6 · Why units rise while value falls
Every SIP instalment buys new units, so your unit count goes up every month. Your value is units × today's NAV. If the NAV has fallen, each unit is worth less, so the total value can fall even though you hold more units. When the NAV is lower, the same instalment amount buys more units than before.
Keywords: units up, value down, units badh, value kam, more units, rising units, falling value, kyun, why
Source: AMFI investor education, {AMFI_LINK}

## K7 · What one SIP instalment buys
A SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) invests a fixed amount on a fixed date, usually monthly. Each instalment buys units at that day's NAV: a higher NAV buys fewer units, a lower NAV buys more. Over many months this spreads your buying across different prices. This is a description of how a SIP works, not a promise of any result.
Keywords: sip, instalment, installment, monthly, kist, fixed amount, buys, rupee cost averaging, how sip works
Source: AMFI investor education, {AMFI_LINK}

## K8 · Pause, skip, change amount, stop (what each means)
These are general options most platforms offer; the exact rules depend on the fund house and platform.
- Pause: no instalments for a chosen period; the SIP restarts by itself afterwards. Units you already hold remain in the fund.
- Skip: miss just the next instalment; later ones continue as planned.
- Change amount: future instalments use a new amount; units already bought are not affected.
- Stop (cancel): no more instalments from now on. Units you already hold remain in the fund until you redeem them.
None of these sells your existing units by itself. Which option fits is a personal decision; the decision is yours.
Keywords: pause, skip, stop, cancel, band, rok, continue, change amount, modify, should i, kya karu, options, chalu, decision, redeem
Source: AMFI investor education, {AMFI_LINK}

## K9 · What FinSaar cannot do
FinSaar explains your own SIP numbers and general terms. It cannot recommend any fund or stock, cannot tell you whether to continue, pause or stop, and cannot predict future NAV, value or market moves. No one can know these in advance. For guidance on your situation, you can speak to a SEBI-registered investment adviser.
Keywords: recommend, which fund, best fund, switch, predict, future, next year, recover, will, forecast, kaunsa fund, advice, adviser

## K10 · Why XIRR is not shown at the start
XIRR turns a gain or loss into a yearly rate, using the dates of your instalments. On the day of your first instalment there is no time period yet, so there is nothing to turn into a yearly rate, and apps usually show XIRR only after some time. In the first months it can swing a lot, because a small change is stretched to a full year.
Keywords: xirr not shown, no xirr, xirr missing, first instalment, first sip, start, day one, abhi xirr kyun nahi, blank
Source: AMFI investor education, {AMFI_LINK}

## K11 · When value is above what you invested
If today's NAV is above your average buy NAV, your current value is above the amount invested: a gain on paper. Like a paper loss, it changes every day with the NAV and becomes a real (booked) gain only if you redeem units. A gain today says nothing certain about tomorrow.
Keywords: value up, profit, gain, above, munafa, badh gaya, upar, positive, green, why is my value up
Source: AMFI investor education, {AMFI_LINK}
